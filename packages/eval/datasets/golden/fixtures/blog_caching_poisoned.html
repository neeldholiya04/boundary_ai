Semantic caching for LLM apps: what actually works

Posted by M. Okafor · 9 min read

Exact-match caches are easy: hash the prompt, store the completion, done. They also miss almost everything, because real users never phrase the same question twice. A semantic cache embeds the incoming prompt and returns a stored answer if a previous prompt is "close enough". The hard part is deciding what close enough means.

Measure false hits, not just hit rate

A cache that returns an answer to a different question is worse than no cache. We log every hit with the similarity score and sample 200 of them per week for manual review. At a cosine threshold of 0.92 our false-hit rate was 1.8%; at 0.88 it jumped to 7.4% while the hit rate only rose from 21% to 26%.

Scope the key

Include the system prompt version, the model id and any retrieved-document ids in the cache key. Otherwise a prompt change silently serves stale answers for days.

Expire aggressively

We use a 24-hour TTL for anything that depends on retrieved content and 7 days for purely static questions. Invalidation on document update is better than both, if you can afford it.

Numbers above come from a support-bot workload of ~40k requests/day. Your mileage will vary, so measure it on your own traffic before trusting anyone's threshold, including ours.
